# Framework sweep — what belongs in Aion

*A pass over the six frameworks above Aion looking for low-level abstractions that
should live at the floor instead. Run 2026-07-29 against `main` at `a2d9956`.*

The instinct behind this sweep is that a stdlib earns its place by **removing** code from
the layers above, not by adding surface of its own. So the question asked of every
candidate was not "is this generic?" but "**is more than one framework solving it, or
conspicuously failing to?**"

---

## The headline: there is almost no duplication to consolidate

Worth stating plainly, because it is the opposite of what a sweep usually finds. Across
`cons`, `mnemosyne`, `elenchon`, `hyperion`, `praxeon`, and `hermes` — 556 top-level
functions — the hand-rolled generic-utility layer is **five functions**: two private
`%split`s (`cons/src/run.lisp`, `hyperion/src/consent.lisp`), a `%join`
(`mnemosyne/src/query.lisp`), two `%drop`s (`mnemosyne/src/ddl.lisp`), and a
`%string-` helper (`praxeon/src/actor.lisp`).

That is a healthy tree, and it means **the case for Aion is not de-duplication.** Pulling
five private one-liners down into a framework dependency would be a net loss. The real
findings are the opposite shape: things *nobody* owns, which the frameworks are each
solving badly, differently, or not at all.

---

## Finding 1 — Two incompatible identity stories, one of them knowingly weak

**`mnemosyne/src/id.lisp`** is 110 lines of careful work: RFC-9562 **v6 UUIDs** with a
**monotonic Gregorian-100ns clock** — a locked, strictly-increasing counter so no two ids
share a timestamp under concurrent burst generation. Its own header notes that
`frugal-uuid`'s default v6 does *not* give this, so the clock is hand-assembled from bit
work with no dependency. The embedded timestamp doubles as a sortable version (`vid`).

**`hyperion/src/session.lisp:114`** mints session identifiers with:

```lisp
(defvar %id-state (make-random-state t))            ; OS-seeded on SBCL
… (random (expt 2 *id-bits*) %id-state)
```

with a comment three lines above acknowledging it should use a CSPRNG "before anything
security-sensitive."

So the tree has a rigorously-built monotonic id generator **locked inside the persistence
framework**, and a security-relevant identifier being minted from `cl:random` in the web
framework. `hyperion` could depend on `mnemosyne` (the DAG allows it), but taking a
persistence framework as a dependency to get a clock is exactly the coupling Aion exists
to prevent — and in any case session tokens want *unpredictability*, which is a different
primitive from *monotonicity*. They are two needs, and neither is at the floor.

**Two Aion modules, deliberately separate:**

- **`aion/clock`** — monotonic time and time-ordered sortable ids. Lift `mnemosyne/id`'s
  clock. This is also what praxeon's Kairos (the bitemporal context engine) will need,
  and Kairos must not depend on mnemosyne to get it.
- **`aion/random`** — a CSPRNG, distinct and clearly labelled. `cl:random` is not one, and
  the distinction should be impossible to get wrong by accident.

Pure CL by nature: clocks and entropy are IO.

## Finding 2 — Retry/backoff is a gap about to become a duplication

`hermes/src/client.lisp:9` says it outright:

> `;;;; NOT YET: retry/redirect (re-running the effect); a first cut sends once.`

Meanwhile `praxeon` has retry concepts across `actor.lisp`, `conditions.lisp`, and
`web-search.lisp`. Both frameworks call flaky remote services — SendGrid and Twilio on one
side, LLM providers and search on the other — and both will need the same thing: bounded
retries, exponential backoff with jitter, and a policy for which conditions are
retryable.

Right now this is *one* implementation and one hole. In three months it is two
implementations that disagree about jitter. **Catching it before the second one is written
is the whole point of running a sweep early**, and it is cheap now in a way it will not be
later.

**`aion/retry`** — a retry *policy as data* (an ADT: attempts, backoff schedule, jitter,
the retryable-condition predicate), with the effectful runner in CL. Policy is pure and
belongs in Coalton; sleeping is IO and does not. A clean small demonstration of the
[CL-half thesis](cl-shell-design.md).

## Finding 3 — Concurrency primitives are spread thin but not obviously wrong

Threads, locks, and queues appear in `hyperion` (`channel`, `session`, `session-db`,
`auth-db`, `dev`, `logging`), `praxeon/web`, `mnemosyne/id`, and `cons/init`. That is a
lot of surface, but the uses are genuinely different — `hyperion/channel` is a broadcast
log with per-reader cursors, `mnemosyne/id` needs one lock around a counter, `dev` needs a
poller thread.

**No action recommended yet.** There is no shared abstraction hiding here that is obvious
from the outside, and inventing one speculatively is how a stdlib gets bloated. Worth
revisiting once `hyperion/channel` has a second consumer — that is the point at which a
real abstraction would reveal itself.

## Finding 4 — `aion/csv` already contains a Clojure-ism worth generalizing

`aion/src/csv/reduced.lisp` implements early-termination for reduction — Clojure's
`reduced`. It is currently private to the CSV reader.

It is a general reduction primitive, and it is exactly the kind of thing the transducer
work ([gap analysis](coalton-gap-analysis.md) §3.3) will need. Worth promoting out of
`csv` when transducers are built, not before — it has one consumer today and generalizing
on a sample size of one is guesswork.

## Summary

| Finding | Action | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Monotonic clock locked in mnemosyne; Kairos will need it too | `aion/clock` — lift `mnemosyne/id` | high |
| Session ids from `cl:random`, flagged weak in a comment | `aion/random` — a real CSPRNG | **high (security)** |
| Retry: one hole (hermes) + one ad-hoc implementation (praxeon) | `aion/retry` — policy as data | high, and cheapest now |
| Concurrency spread across frameworks | none — revisit at a second consumer | — |
| `reduced` private to the CSV reader | promote alongside transducers | low |
| Generic string/list helpers | **none — five private functions, leave them** | — |

The three "high" items share a property worth noticing: **none of them is a Coalton gap.**
They are clocks, entropy, and sleep — IO, all of it. The gap analysis found what Coalton
lacks; this sweep found what *Common Lisp* should be giving the tree and currently is not.
Together they are the two halves of what Aion is for.
