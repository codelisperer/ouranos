\begin{abstract}
Common Lisp's built-in sequence handling is notoriously inconsistent---lists,
vectors, and extensible sequences each demand different accessors, and mutation is
the default. The Clojure community, by contrast, made \emph{immutable, persistent}
collections and a single sequence abstraction the substrate of an entire language.
We ask how to bring that ``Clojure goodness'' to Common Lisp without abandoning
CL, and find that much of the hard work is already done: \textsc{Coalton}, a
statically typed, Hindley--Milner language embedded in CL, already ships persistent
RRB-tree vectors, HAMT maps, ordered maps, immutable lists, a lazy iterator, and a
full typeclass tower \emph{including monad transformers}. We present \textsc{Aion},
a library that (i) exposes this substrate to plain-CL developers through a
consistent, generic sequence protocol---finally giving Common Lisp a coherent
immutable-collections story---and (ii) fills the genuine remaining gaps: persistent
sets, transducers, optics, memoized lazy sequences, and threading macros. Aion is part of a six-framework ecosystem whose thesis is to
establish Coalton as a first-class ``modern Lisp,'' a peer to Clojure, Scheme,
OCaml, and Haskell.
\end{abstract}
