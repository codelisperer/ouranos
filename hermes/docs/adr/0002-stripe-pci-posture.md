# ADR-0002 — Stripe integration: hosted Checkout only, and the PCI posture that follows

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-02) · 2026-08-27 · issues #47, #50

> **Not compliance advice.** This records an engineering decision and the reasoning behind
> it. PCI scope is determined by your acquirer and your own attestation, and Stripe's
> Dashboard is where the applicable SAQ is issued. Where this ADR states a fact about
> Stripe, it cites Stripe. Where it could not confirm one, it says so.

## Context

The maintainer's understanding — *"Stripe manages PCI by giving us some kind of token and we
never actually see bank information"* — is **substantially correct**, and it is worth
recording precisely, because the two places it is imprecise are the two that decide how much
work we have.

What Stripe states directly:

- Low-risk integrations **"securely collect and transmit payment information directly to
  Stripe without it passing through your servers, reducing your PCI obligations."**
- Card type, **last four digits**, and expiration date **"isn't subject to PCI compliance,
  so you can store any of these properties in your database"** — along with anything else
  the API returns.
- **"Sensitive information is never included in a webhook event."**
- Stripe Checkout **"allows you to qualify for the simplest form of PCI validation, a
  prefilled SAQ A."**

So: the card number (PAN) never touches our servers, and what we hold is a **reference**
(`cus_…`, `sub_…`, `pi_…`) plus non-sensitive descriptors. That is the token model, and it
is real.

**Two corrections.** First, *"avoid PCI issues altogether"* is not achievable and should not
be the goal: PCI is **a shared responsibility** and a merchant **"must annually attest"**.
SAQ A is *minimal* scope, not *no* scope — the win is avoiding the 300+ controls that apply
when untokenized PANs reach your systems, not avoiding assessment.

Second, **hosted Checkout and Elements are not equivalent**, and #50's checklist currently
lumps them ("Use hosted Checkout + Elements only"). Checkout redirects to a Stripe-hosted
page; Elements embeds Stripe-controlled iframes in **our** page, which makes our page part of
the payment flow. Both keep the PAN off our servers. They do **not** obviously carry the same
assessment, and we could not confirm the current SAQ A / SAQ A-EP boundary for Elements under
PCI DSS v4 from Stripe's public documentation.

## Decision

**Hosted Checkout only. Elements is out of scope until deliberately adopted.**

1. **The protocol accepts no card data, by shape rather than by policy.** There is
   deliberately no operation, and no argument, through which a PAN could be passed. A rule
   can be followed incorrectly; an absent operation cannot. This is what holds scope at the
   Checkout case.
2. **We store references and non-sensitive descriptors only** — customer/subscription/payment
   refs, and if ever needed, brand/last4/expiry, which Stripe states are out of scope.
3. **Elements is not implemented**, and adopting it later is an explicit decision requiring
   its own ADR — because it changes what our page is, and therefore what we must attest to.
4. **Webhook signatures are verified before the payload is parsed.** Nothing in an unverified
   payload is worth reading, including its type.
5. **Idempotency keys are generated by default** on create calls, not left to a caller who
   must remember.
6. **Restricted keys only**, via git-ignored `.env` through `cons`, documented in
   `.env.example`.

## Consequences

- The checkout experience is a **redirect**, not an embedded form. That is a product
  constraint accepted knowingly: it is the cheapest defensible scope, and it is reversible
  by an ADR rather than by an afternoon.
- Our attestation path is the simplest SAQ Stripe offers, and we still complete one annually.
- **Two obligations land outside this library and must not be assumed** because hermes
  cannot discharge them: Stripe advises **allowlisting Stripe's IP ranges** in addition to
  signature verification, and **`Stripe.js` must be loaded directly from `js.stripe.com`**,
  never bundled or self-hosted. Both are deployment and page concerns. If Elements is ever
  adopted, the CSP directives in Stripe's integration security guide become ours too.
- Payment pages require **TLS 1.2 or above**.

## Alternatives considered

**Elements now, for a better checkout experience.** Rejected for v1 on the basis that we
could not confirm its current assessment boundary, and an unconfirmed compliance assumption
is the wrong kind of unknown to build a payment flow on. Revisit deliberately, with an ADR.

**Direct card APIs.** Rejected outright — this is the 300-control path, and no product
requirement here justifies entering it.

**Storing card metadata for display.** Permitted by Stripe (brand/last4/expiry are out of
scope) but not implemented, because it is not needed: the hosted portal shows the customer
their own payment methods.

## Provenance

Written because the maintainer asked whether his understanding of the token model was
correct. It was — and checking rather than confirming from memory surfaced two things that
would otherwise have been missed: that **#50's checklist treats Checkout and Elements as
equivalent when they may not be**, and that **IP allowlisting and `js.stripe.com` script
loading are obligations this library cannot discharge for its consumers**, so they have to be
written down somewhere a consumer will read.

The boundary this ADR could **not** resolve from public documentation — SAQ A versus
SAQ A-EP for Elements under PCI DSS v4, and the applicability of requirements 6.4.3 and
11.6.1 — is recorded as unresolved rather than guessed. It is the reason Elements is
deferred rather than the reason it is rejected.
